# Endpoint Detection and Response (EDR) in SOC

## What is EDR?

Endpoint Detection and Response (EDR) is a cybersecurity technology used in **Security Operations Centers (SOC)**. It continuously monitors endpoints (like laptops, desktops, and servers) to detect, analyze, and respond to suspicious activities or cyber threats **in real time**.

EDR tools help security teams quickly identify attacks, investigate their root cause, and take action to contain and fix the problem before it spreads.

---

## How EDR Works

EDR solutions follow these main steps:

1. **Continuous Monitoring** – Keep track of all endpoint activities 24/7.
2. **Threat Detection** – Identify suspicious behavior or known attack patterns.
3. **Alert Generation** – Notify the SOC team when threats are detected.
4. **Incident Investigation** – Analyze alerts to find out what happened and how severe it is.
5. **Response and Remediation** – Contain, remove, or fix the threat to protect the system.
6. **Threat Intelligence Integration** – Use global threat data to stay updated on new attack methods.

---

## Why EDR is Essential in SOC

* **Real-Time Response**: Stops threats before they spread.
* **Proactive Threat Hunting**: Helps analysts find hidden threats before they cause damage.
* **Complete Visibility**: Gives full insight into endpoint activities and attacks.
* **Post-Attack Analysis**: Allows investigation and learning from past incidents to improve defenses.

---

## Example to Understand

**Imagine you’re working in an office:**

* You receive a suspicious email with an attachment.
* Without knowing, you open it and a malicious program starts running silently in the background.
* The EDR tool detects this unusual activity instantly (like unknown file execution, data exfiltration, etc.).
* It immediately alerts the SOC team and automatically blocks the file’s activity.
* Analysts investigate the incident, find the source, remove the malware, and update defenses to prevent similar attacks in the future.

---

### Conclusion

EDR is a **critical layer** of modern cybersecurity. It ensures organizations can **detect, respond, and learn** from attacks quickly, minimizing damage and improving overall security posture.
