🔐 1. Threats, Vulnerabilities, and Risks – Explained
Threat: A potential attack or harmful action that can damage systems, steal data, or disrupt services.
👉 Examples: Ransomware, phishing emails, malicious file downloads.

Vulnerability: A flaw or weakness in the system that attackers can exploit.
👉 Examples: Using default passwords, outdated software, or weak security configurations.

Risk: The possibility of a threat successfully exploiting a vulnerability.
👉 Formula: Risk = Threat + Vulnerability

🕵️‍♂️ 2. IoC vs IoA (Indicators)
IoC (Indicator of Compromise)
➤ Shows an attack has already happened.
➤ Helps in identifying traces/footprints of the attacker.
👉 Example: Detection of malware files, communication with a command-and-control server.

IoA (Indicator of Attack)
➤ Indicates that an attack might be in progress.
➤ Helps in proactive monitoring and detection.
👉 Example: Multiple failed login attempts from unknown IPs, unusual user behavior in logs.

📌 These concepts are helping me understand how to think like a SOC Analyst—spotting weak points, understanding threats, and detecting attacks proactively or after they happen.
